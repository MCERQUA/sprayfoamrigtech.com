A spray foam rig is a working piece of industrial equipment, not a set-it-and-forget-it tool — and the maintenance schedule that keeps it reliable isn't complicated, just consistent. Here's a practical breakdown of what needs attention and how often.

## Daily / Pre-Shift

- **Visual hose inspection** — check for cracks, kinks, or wear along the full length of the heated hoses, especially at connection points where stress concentrates.
- **Filter check** — a quick look at the in-line filters on both the A and B sides catches early clogging before it affects ratio.
- **Fluid level check** — confirm both chemical drums have adequate supply and that lines are properly primed before starting.

## Weekly

- **Filter changes** — depending on usage volume and material, weekly filter replacement on both sides is a common baseline (adjust based on your specific manufacturer's recommendation and how heavily the rig is used).
- **Gun cleaning** — a thorough clean of the spray gun, not just a quick rinse, prevents buildup that affects spray pattern and mixing at the tip.
- **Generator check** — fluid levels, filter condition, and a general run check on the power source that the whole rig depends on.

## Monthly

- **Pump seal inspection** — seals wear gradually, and catching early wear during a scheduled check is far less disruptive than discovering it mid-job as an off-ratio problem.
- **Calibration verification** — confirming the proportioner's actual output ratio against its target, not just trusting that the gauges look right.
- **Full hose system check** — beyond the daily visual, a more thorough inspection of heating elements and insulation along the hose length.

## Annually (or Per Manufacturer Schedule)

- **Full proportioner service** — many manufacturers specify an annual (or hours-based) full service interval covering pumps, heaters, and internal components that aren't practical to inspect on a daily or weekly basis.
- **Generator major service** — oil changes, filter replacement, and a full inspection consistent with the generator manufacturer's schedule.

## Why This Schedule, Not a Looser One

Every item on this list connects back to the same underlying risk: a small, gradual equipment issue that goes unnoticed until it shows up as off-ratio foam on a real job. The daily and weekly items are cheap and fast — a few minutes that catch the vast majority of problems before they cost a callback. The monthly and annual items are where the more expensive, harder-to-catch issues (seal wear, calibration drift) get addressed before they become field failures.

## The Real Cost of Skipping It

A rig that's "running fine" can mask developing issues for weeks before a problem surfaces — and by the time it does, it's usually mid-job, not on a convenient schedule. Contractors who stick to a real maintenance schedule aren't just protecting their equipment investment; they're protecting every job that equipment touches.
